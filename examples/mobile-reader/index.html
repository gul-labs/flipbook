Mara found a smooth stone where the river bent around the willows.

She sat with the stone in both hands and read the river the way other people read a letter. Each eddy was a comma. Each snag of reed was a pause she could almost hear. Downstream a heron waited as if the next sentence had to be earned, and the long afternoon kept offering the same paragraph again: water over rock, rock under water, light breaking on the surface without ever belonging to it. Mara turned the stone and the story did not become a picture of a page. It stayed words, wet and particular, waiting for a voice that was not here yet.
